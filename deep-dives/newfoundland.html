Temperament

Newfoundlands are gentle, patient, and famously good with children — earning a well-deserved "nanny dog" reputation. They're calm and loyal, with a natural affinity for water and swimming rooted in their history as water rescue dogs.

Health & Lifespan

Newfoundlands typically live 8–10 years. Hip and elbow dysplasia are common. Subvalvular aortic stenosis, a heart condition, is a specific concern in the breed. Bloat is a real risk given size, and cystinuria, a hereditary condition affecting kidney and bladder stone formation, is documented in the breed.

Nutrition Needs

Large-breed puppy formula matters during growth, along with ongoing joint support and bloat precautions given their size. Some Newfoundlands benefit from specific dietary management around cystinuria risk — worth a direct conversation with a vet given how breed-specific this condition is.

Family Fit

Newfoundlands are exceptional with kids, patient and protective without aggression. Drooling and heavy shedding are simply part of daily life, and this breed does best with real space and cooler climates given its heavy coat.

The Honest Take

The "nanny dog" reputation is genuinely earned — this really is one of the most naturally gentle giant breeds around children. But the coat, the drool, the heat sensitivity, and the giant-breed health costs are all real and substantial, and good temperament alone doesn't offset them.
