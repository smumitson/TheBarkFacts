Temperament

Bichons are cheerful, affectionate, and genuinely people-oriented — this is a breed built for companionship, not work. They're playful and generally good-natured, though like many small breeds, inconsistent structure can lead to anxious or overly demanding behavior if boundaries aren't established early.

Health & Lifespan

Bichons are relatively long-lived, typically 14–15 years. Allergies and skin issues appear commonly in the breed. Dental problems are frequent in small breeds generally, and Bichons are no exception — crowded teeth need real attention. Luxating patella (kneecap issues) and bladder stones also appear at notable rates.

Nutrition Needs

A quality small-breed formula suits this dog well, and dental health is closely tied to diet given how prone small breeds are to crowding and buildup. Portion control matters since Bichons are food-motivated but not especially active, making weight creep an easy trap.

Family Fit

Bichons do well with gentle kids and adapt easily to apartment living given their low exercise needs. Early socialization helps prevent the small-dog anxiety and yappiness that can develop without structure. This is a breed that wants to be with people constantly — not a good fit for owners gone long hours regularly.

The Honest Take

The real commitment with this breed isn't behavior or health — it's the coat. Regular professional grooming isn't optional; that soft, curly coat mats quickly without consistent maintenance. People see "small, fluffy, friendly" and assume low-maintenance, when in reality this is one of the higher grooming-upkeep breeds on this list.
