Temperament

Border Collies are widely considered the most intelligent dog breed, and that intelligence shows up as intense focus and drive rather than easy-going companionship. Herding instinct runs deep — many Border Collies will "eye" and stalk household members, kids, cars, or other pets the same way they'd work sheep.

This is a breed that needs constant engagement. Without it, that famous intelligence turns inward into obsessive or anxious behaviors.

Health & Lifespan

Border Collies typically live 12–15 years and are a relatively healthy breed for their size. Hip dysplasia, collie eye anomaly, and epilepsy all appear in the breed. Deafness shows up in some lines, particularly those with significant white coloring, and is worth screening for with a breeder.

Nutrition Needs

Working-line Border Collies with genuine daily herding or sport demands need real caloric density to match their output. Pet-line dogs getting normal exercise need considerably less — matching food to actual activity level matters more with this breed than assuming size dictates portions.

Family Fit

Border Collies thrive with active families who can genuinely match their drive — agility, herding, flyball, or serious daily structured activity. The herding nip needs consistent redirection around younger kids. This is not a breed suited to long hours alone or a sedentary household.

The Honest Take

The intelligence that makes this breed so appealing is also the trap. A Border Collie without real mental and physical work becomes obsessive, anxious, and sometimes destructive — not because something's wrong with the dog, but because nothing is being asked of a mind built to work constantly.

If you want a true working partner and can provide the outlet this breed needs, a Border Collie is genuinely extraordinary. If you want "smart dog" without the demands that come with it, this is the wrong breed for that expectation.
