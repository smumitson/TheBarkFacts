Temperament

Dachshunds carry a huge personality in a small, elongated body — bold, curious, and stubborn in a way that catches new owners off guard. They're vocal, often quick to bark, and can be feisty toward strangers or unfamiliar dogs. Loyalty to family runs deep.

Health & Lifespan

Dachshunds typically live 12–16 years. Intervertebral disc disease (IVDD) is the breed's defining health risk, a direct consequence of the long back and short legs — this isn't a rare complication, it's a central consideration for the breed. Obesity significantly compounds IVDD risk. Dental issues, patellar luxation, and eye conditions also appear commonly.

Nutrition Needs

Weight management is the single most important nutrition factor for this breed — it's directly tied to spinal health given their proportions. Avoiding stairs and jumping, and keeping portions strictly controlled, matter more here than food brand choice.

Family Fit

Dachshunds do well with respectful kids who understand not to roughhouse, given how fragile that long back really is. They can be territorial and protective of their people, and they adapt well to apartment living given moderate exercise needs.

The Honest Take

Like the Corgi, weight and jumping are the two factors that determine whether this dog lives a comfortable life or a painful one. Ramps, strict portion control, and a no-couch-jumping house rule should be non-negotiable from day one, not an afterthought once problems appear.
