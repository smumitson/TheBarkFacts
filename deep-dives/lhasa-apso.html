Temperament

Lhasa Apsos were bred as indoor sentinels in Tibetan monasteries, and that watchful, alert instinct is still very present. They're loyal to family but genuinely wary of strangers, independent-minded, and carry a stubborn streak that surprises people expecting a purely decorative lap dog.

Health & Lifespan

Lhasa Apsos typically live 12–15 years. Eye conditions, including progressive retinal atrophy and dry eye, appear commonly. A specific hereditary kidney condition has been documented in the breed. Patellar luxation is common, though hip dysplasia is less prevalent here than in many small breeds.

Nutrition Needs

A small-breed formula with careful weight management suits this breed given its low activity level. Standard dental care applies, and there's no major additional dietary flag beyond general small-breed considerations.

Family Fit

Lhasa Apsos do well in calm households with respectful kids. Their watchful, alert nature means some real wariness of visitors is normal — this isn't automatically the friendliest lap dog, and socialization matters for a well-adjusted adult.

The Honest Take

The flowing coat gets all the attention, but underneath is a genuinely independent, watchful little dog with real guarding instincts left over from its monastery-sentinel job — not simply a decorative lap dog. The grooming commitment is also one of the most demanding on this entire list.

The Lhasa Apso is a small breed.

The Lhasa Apso has very high grooming needs.

The Lhasa Apso is independent-minded, which can make training more challenging.

Is the Lhasa Apso a good choice for first-time owners?

Yes — the Lhasa Apso is generally considered a good fit for first-time dog owners.
